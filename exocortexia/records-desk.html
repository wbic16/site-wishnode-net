Records Desk

Public-records search and redaction · Neb. Rev. Stat. § 84-712 et seq.

What it does

A custodian searches public-records documents, applies statutory redactions, and produces an export packet for the requester. The demo ships with a corpus of roughly 400 synthetic documents; the storage layer underneath is separately proven against a generated 100,000-document corpus (see the suite's verification receipts).

  • Redaction is a layer, never an edit. A redaction is stored as separate, reviewable data; the original scroll is never modified. The redacted view is computed at read time, and a custodian view shows the untouched original bytes side by side.
  • Statutory categories only. The redact form offers exactly the record categories § 84-712.05 lists as withholdable — a redaction without a valid statutory category is rejected.
  • The worksheet is the point. The export packet's time-and-cost estimate is explicit arithmetic — pages from bytes, minutes from pages, cost from a stated hourly rate, delivery in working days. Search, review, and arithmetic do not take years.
  • Everything is audited. Searches, redactions, and exports append to the suite's hash-chained audit log.

The context for this demo is real: in October 2026, Nebraska DHHS quoted a requester $976,263.77 and a delivery date in the year 2076 for public-records emails, a quote now the subject of a Public Records Act lawsuit. Records Desk exists to show what the same workflow looks like when the arithmetic is transparent and the search is instant.

Statute mapping

Neb. Rev. Stat. § 84-712 et seq. (public records). § 84-712.05 enumerates the categories of records that may be withheld; those categories are the redaction categories the application offers. The full mapping, with template RFP language, is in the repository: docs/records-desk-statute.md.
